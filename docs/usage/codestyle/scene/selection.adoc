# The selection chain

**TL;DR**

* One `SelectionSystem` per scene, on the `SceneContent`, acquired the way
  `DragSystem` is (`acquireForNode`). **One selection at a time, scene-wide**:
  selecting into a different container clears the previous one wholesale.
* A selection is an **owner** plus the **items it handed out**. Items are opaque
  `SelectionItem`s — an `Rc<Ref>` identity and an index — and **never a `Node *`**:
  rows are virtualized, so the selected row may have no node at all.
* A container joins by implementing **`SelectionOwner`** and being told to:
  `TreeView`/`TableView::setSelectionOwned(true)`. **Opt-in**, because the same
  widget is what `ui::SearchPicker` and `ui::Select` put inside a popup.
* CSS: **`:selected`** on the item, **`:selection-within`** on every ancestor up
  to the root. Both come from a marker component, not from interactive state.
* Hotkeys are offered **along the chain, deepest first** — the row, its parents,
  then the container — before the ordinary walk. **`HotkeyFlags::SelectedOnly`**
  restricts a binding to that pass.
* **Nothing is derived from focus.** Selection is set by an explicit call and by
  nothing else; "focusing me selects me" is one line in a widget that wants it.
* **Arrows move the selection** (`SelectionSystem::moveSelection`): first inside the
  owner, then to the closest node registered with **`setNodeSelectable`** that was
  drawn in that direction. They reach the system only when the ordinary key route
  did not take them.
* **A press may select** (`SelectionSystem::setSelectOnPress`, off by default): the
  topmost selectable node under the pointer, leaving rows to their owner's own tap.

## Why it exists

The engine could already say *where typing goes*. It could not say *what the user
is working on* — and three things need that answer: which listener gets a hotkey
and in what order, which hotkeys may fire at all, and what the whole hierarchy
looks like in CSS.

Before this, a selection was a `size_t` index private to one widget, with no
scene-wide meaning; `TreeView` never remapped it on a rebuild, so expanding a
category above the selected row silently moved the selection onto a different
element. Two tables that both had a selected row were resolved by nothing but
paint order. And Ctrl+Z reached the right history only because
`TextInputManager` allows one IME handler per window.

## Selecting

[source,cpp]
----
auto system = SelectionSystem::acquireForNode(this);

// A container with items of its own
SelectionItem item{modelNode, spanOffset};
system->select(this, makeSpanView(&item, 1));

// A plain node that IS its own identity - a canvas object, a card, a panel
system->selectNode(node);

system->clear();
----

`select()` answers `false` when nothing changed, so a widget may call it on every
tap without checking first.

## Identity, not index, and not a node

`SelectionItem` is `{Rc<Ref> ref; uint64_t index;}`, and both halves are needed. A
`TreeView` row is `(ModelNode, offset)`: a `Kind::Span` node stands for many rows
that share its `ItemId` and differ only by offset. An `ItemId` is allocated once
and never reused, which is what makes the pair survive a rebuild — an insertion or
a removal anywhere cannot make an identity mean a different element.

**A node is a projection of an identity, recomputed every frame**, and it may be
absent: a row scrolled out of its window, or inside a collapsed category, has no
node while remaining perfectly selected. `SelectionOwner::resolveSelectionNode`
answers `null` for those, and is asked once per item per frame — keep it to a map
lookup, and never let it *build* the node. A selection is not a reason to
materialize a row the user cannot see.

This is also why the **owner** is recorded and not just the items: when nothing is
materialized, the owner is what the chain anchors on, so a hotkey still reaches
the container's undo history.

## The chain

`getChain()` is the anchor and every ancestor up to the scene root, deepest first.
The anchor is:

* the single item's node, when there is **exactly one** item and it is
  materialized;
* the **owner's** node otherwise — which covers both a recycled row and a
  multi-item selection. Anchoring a multi-item selection on an arbitrary one of
  its items would offer that row's own listener a chord the other selected rows
  never see; a multi-item operation belongs to the container.

**The chain is published into the committed frame** (`InputListenerStorage::setSelectionChain`)
during the visit, and the dispatcher reads it from there. It is never re-derived
at event time: a hotkey callback is entitled to restructure the scene, up to and
including deleting the node it was reached through, so the walk has to be over
`Rc`s taken from the frame the user was looking at when they pressed the key.

Consequence: a selection that changes during a commit reaches the storage **one
frame later**, the same lag `:focus-within` and the hit-test registry already
have. Do not "fix" it by re-publishing after the commit — that would make the
storage describe a frame nobody drew.

## Releasing the marker: keep the chain, never re-walk it

`updateSelectionChain` takes **collected chains**, not anchors, and this is not a
stylistic choice:

[source,cpp]
----
Vector<Rc<Node>> next;
buildSelectionChain(anchor, next);
updateSelectionChain(_chain, next);   // retains `next` BEFORE releasing `_chain`
_chain = sp::move(next);
----

Releasing by walking `getParent()` from the old anchor reads the graph at a moment
that is not the moment it was retained — and in the ordinary virtualized case the
anchor has been **detached** in between. The walk stops at the detached node and
every ancestor above it keeps a count nothing will ever release, so a container
stays `:selection-within` forever with nothing selected in it.

Retain-before-release is the other half: a shared ancestor of the two chains goes
`1 -> 2 -> 1` and never loses its component, so neither it nor anything under it is
restyled for a move that never left it.

## CSS

[source,css]
----
tree-row:selected          { background-color: #094771; }
tree-view:selection-within { outline-color: #3d7ecf; outline-width: 1px; }
----

`:selected` is **not** the `.selected` class and does not replace it. The class is
written by `TreeView`, `TableView` and `ui::Chip` to mean "my own current row",
and two of those are not the scene selection at all — a chip that is on, a nav tab
that is the current page. A tree row simply wears both.

Both pseudo-classes come from `SelectionComponent`, a marker component, for the
reason `FocusWithinComponent` is one: `InteractiveComponent` defaults to
`Enabled`, so writing the bit there would switch `:enabled` on for a plain
container and `:disabled` off, and only while something happened to be selected.

There is no `:unselected` and no `:not-selection-within`: the negative would match
every node in the scene.

## Hotkeys along the chain

`InputDispatcher::handleHotkey` gains one pass between the keyboard owner and the
ordinary walk: the chain, **deepest first**, with the normal walk order breaking
ties within one chain node. A chain listener that declines is not offered the key
again below — declining is an answer.

`HotkeyFlags::SelectedOnly` restricts a binding to that pass. It **narrows who is
offered** the chord and nothing more: the engine contract is still that a handler
with nothing to do returns `false`, so `TableView`'s reorder keys carry the flag
*and* keep their own check that there is a row to move.

A binding with `SelectedOnly` on a view that has not opted in is never offered
anything, so `TableView` adds the flag only while `isSelectionOwned()`.

## Focus

**Nothing here watches focus, and no engine rule ties the two.** The coupling is
one call in the widget that wants it. `ui::TextInput` and `ui::FormSystem` make it
when a field takes focus, through `SelectionSystem::selectEnclosing`: the nearest
selectable ancestor without an owner (the panel the field is in) is selected,
unless the selection already runs through it. Where typing goes is then also what
is selected, and a dock frame outline (see `ui/docking.adoc`) shows it. A widget of
your own does the same:

[source,cpp]
----
listener->setFocusCallback([this](bool focused) {
    if (focused) {
        if (auto system = SelectionSystem::findForNode(this)) {
            system->selectNode(this);
        }
    }
    // losing focus does NOT clear the selection - see below
});
----

An engine-enforced "focus lives inside the selection" cannot be written, for three
independent reasons:

. `commitStorage` commits **every** `FocusGroup` in the frame. A scene routinely
  has several — a form, a dock panel, a menu popup — each with its own focused
  listener, so there is no single "the focus" to be inside anything.
. `ui::FormSystem` answers `true` for **every** listener while nothing is focused,
  so "focus is outside the selection" is not decidable.
. `FocusGroup::setFocus` only records `_nextListener`; the swap fires inside
  `commitStorage`, after the visit. A selection reacting to a focus commit would
  publish its answer one commit later, every time.

The coupling is deliberately **asymmetric**: taking focus may select, losing focus
must not clear. Dropping focus opens `FormSystem`'s gate for every form listener,
so treating a blur as "nothing is current any more" would clear the selection
whenever a field was merely left.

`select()` re-entered from inside a change callback is applied by a loop after the
current delivery returns — never on top of it — and a request equal to the current
state is dropped before any callback runs. That stops `A -> A`. It does **not**
stop two owners selecting each other, because neither request ever equals the
current state, so the loop is bounded at `MaxRedirects` and says so in the log.

## Arrow navigation

[source,cpp]
----
// A plain node the arrows may select
setNodeSelectable(card, true);

// A container: the arrows enter it through its SelectionOwner
setNodeSelectable(this, true, this);

system->moveSelection(SelectionDirection::Down);
----

`SelectionSystem` subscribes its own listener to `EngineHotkeys::selectLeft`,
`selectRight`, `selectUp` and `selectDown` with `HotkeyFlags::Unhandled |
Repeatable`, so a key reaches it only after the ordinary key route declined it.
A focused `ui::Slider`, `ui::ChipRow`, `ui::Select`, a menu or a text view keeps
its arrows; the selection moves when nothing else wanted them. A modal dialog's
`Exclusive` group scopes the pass as usual.

One step is:

. **Inside the owner.** `SelectionOwner::moveSelection(dir)` — `true` when it
  handled the key, `false` at its edge. Both new owner methods have defaults that
  answer `false`, so an owner that knows nothing about arrows is simply left.
. **Into a container inside a plain node.** When a plain node is selected (a dock
  frame, a panel), each selectable owner drawn inside it is offered
  `enterSelection`, topmost first. A selected panel therefore hands the first
  arrow to its tree or table instead of jumping to the next panel.
. **Otherwise by geometry.** The source is the anchor's rect as it was drawn. The
  candidates are the committed frame's hit-test records with
  `HitTestFlags::Selectable`, clipped by their scissor, excluding the owner node,
  its ancestors and its descendants. A candidate must lie entirely past the source's
  near edge (1px tolerance). Candidates that overlap the source across the
  direction win; among the rest `13 * major² + minor²` decides
  (`getSelectionDirectionScore`).
. **Entering.** A plain node is selected with `selectNode`. A node registered with
  an owner is offered `SelectionOwner::enterSelection(dir, fromWorld)`; a refusal
  passes to the next candidate.

Directions are physical: Left is left in a right-to-left window too. What they
*mean* inside an owner is the owner's business. `ui::TreeView` steps rows on
Up/Down, and on the inline-start arrow collapses an open category or goes to the
parent, on the inline-end arrow expands a closed one or enters its first child —
both follow `isInlineRtl`. `ui::TableView` steps rows on Up/Down and leaves on
Left/Right. Both register themselves while `isSelectionOwned()`, scroll the new
row into view, report it through the select callback like a tap, and enter on
the closest visible row. Inside that callback `isSelectingFromKeyboard()` is
`true`, so a list that opens what is picked waits for the activation instead:
while it owns the selection, Enter (`formSubmit`, `SelectedOnly`) calls the
activate callback on the selected row, as a double tap does.

Only **drawn** nodes are candidates: a node scrolled out of its clip, hidden or
not yet visited is not in the registry. There is no generic scroll-into-view for
plain nodes, so arrows do not scroll a list of cards past what is shown; a
virtualized owner scrolls itself.

## Mirroring a selection made elsewhere

A list that shows what another surface selected (an outline beside a canvas, a
table following the document) moves its row with `showSelectedRow`, not
`setSelectedRow`. The row moves either way; `showSelectedRow` hands it to the
`SelectionSystem` only while the view already holds the selection, so the mirror
never takes the keyboard away from the canvas the author clicked. A tap on a row,
including the one already selected, always takes the selection.

## Selecting on press

[source,cpp]
----
SelectionSystem::acquireForNode(root)->setSelectOnPress(true);
----

With it on, a pointer press (any of the three buttons) is observed by a listener
that runs ahead of the scene and never takes the event. It looks up the topmost
drawn node registered with `setNodeSelectable` under the pointer:

* nothing happens when the selection already runs through that node, so a press
  anywhere inside the selected panel keeps a row selected in it;
* a node registered **with an owner** is left to the owner, which selects its row
  on its own tap. When the selection is outside the owner, its nearest selectable
  ancestor without an owner is selected first, so a press on an empty part of a
  list still moves the selection to the panel holding it;
* any other node is selected with `selectNode`.

A tap is reported only once the double-tap window has passed, so the row replaces
the panel a moment after the press. A check that clicks a row polls for the row.

The option is off by default because it changes what a click means for the whole
scene: an application that marks its panels selectable turns it on once.

## Traps

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Trap | What actually happens

| Putting the bits on `InteractiveComponent`
| It defaults to `Enabled`, so a plain container given one starts matching
  `:enabled` and stops matching `:disabled`.

| Releasing the chain before retaining the new one
| A shared ancestor loses its component for an instant and its whole subtree is
  restyled for a move that never left it.

| Releasing by walking `getParent()`
| The anchor may already be detached, and every counter above it is stranded. Keep
  the chain you retained.

| Addressing a row by `Node *` or by index
| Rows are virtualized and indices shift on every rebuild. Both are how a
  selection ends up on a different element with nothing to say it happened.

| Dropping the identity when no row matches
| "Hidden" and "gone" arrive here identically and need the same answer: keep the
  identity, drop only the index. Re-expanding a category then brings the selection
  back, and a stale `ItemId` simply never matches again.

| Making a popup's list a selection owner
| `ui::SearchPicker` and `ui::Select` drive `setSelectedRow` on lists of their own.
  A popup that joined the scene selection would silently discard the
  application's. Participation is opt-in per instance.

| Expecting a selected row to have a listener
| A `TreeView` row gets an `InputListener` only when selection is enabled, and
  even then it carries only hover and tap. "The element's own listener receives
  the hotkey" is a contract the application opts into by subscribing on the row.

| Registering a view that sits inside a selectable node
| `ui::MarkdownView` is not a candidate on purpose: inside a selectable bubble it
  would compete with the bubble for the same arrow. Register the outermost thing
  the user means.

| Expecting arrows to reach the selection past a focused widget
| `HotkeyFlags::Unhandled`: the key route goes first. A widget that captures every
  key while focused hides the arrows from the selection until it is blurred.

| `Node::cleanup()` and the markers
| `removeFromParent()` defaults to `cleanup()`, which calls `removeAllComponents()`
  — taking the selection markers *and* the node's name, which is also a component.
|===

## Verification

`XL_SELECTION_TEST` (`widgets/selection`) and `tests/window/selection-check.py`.
Arrow navigation: `XL_SELECTION_NAV_TEST` (`widgets/selection-nav`) and
`tests/window/selection-nav-check.py`, which sends real arrow keys.
The stand covers the system and the markers; the driver covers everything that
only exists across frames — the chain spelled out deepest-first, the delivery
order of a chord along it, and that holding one selection still for a hundred
frames restyles nothing.

`XL_STATE_TEST` group 7b drives the two markers directly, with no system in the
middle, so that the component, the two `InteractiveFlags` bits, the selector
parser and the five fold-in sites are asserted to agree before anything is built
on top.
