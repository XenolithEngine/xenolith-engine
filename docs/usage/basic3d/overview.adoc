# The 3d layer (basic3d)

A depth-tested 3d scene drawn under the basic2d UI of the same window: meshes with mip-mapped textures, an ambient light, a sun and point lights, seen through a camera that can be the isometric game camera or a perspective one.

**TL;DR**

- The scene is a [`basic3d::Scene3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3dScene.h) with a [`basic3d::SceneContent3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3dSceneContent.h); any number of [`basic3d::View3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3dView.h) nodes in it show 3d views, and every basic2d node works as before, over them.
- A view draws [`Draw3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3d.h)s: a [`Mesh3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3dMesh.h), a transform and a `Material3d` (a colour times an optional `Texture`, tiled `uvScale` times).
- The [`Camera`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3dCamera.h) is plain math: a target, a yaw, an elevation and a distance; orthographic in points per unit (times the density) or perspective; depth in [0, 1], clip y flipped for Vulkan. `project`, `unproject`, `screenRay`, `screenToGround` and `projectAabb` work in the scene's pixels, y up - the space of input locations and of `Node::getWorldBoundingBox`.
- An app can add pipelines of its own: `Scene3d::init` takes a list of [`Pipeline3dInfo`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3d.h) (SPIR-V vertex and fragment shaders over the pass's interface), and a material picks one by `pipeline`, with `flags`, four `params` and a second and a third texture, `texture2` and `texture3`, for its shaders.
- Point lights: [`Light3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3d.h) - a position, a radius and a colour times intensity, falling off as `(1 - (d / r)^2)^2`. `View3d::addLight`, or `ViewInput3d::lights` from an overriding `collectDraws`; up to `MaxLights` (64) a view, the rest dropped with a warning. The mesh shader lights by them; an app shader reads them through `Constants3d::lightPointer` and the view's `firstLight`/`lightCount`.
- Meshes can live in GPU memory between frames: a [`MeshPack3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3dMeshPack.h) puts a batch of meshes in one vertex and one index buffer of a temporary resource. While a view retains it (its own draws do by themselves; `View3d::retainPack`/`releasePack` otherwise), the pass draws those meshes from it and copies nothing into the frame; with no one retaining it, it is cleared and leaves the resource cache.
- Textures sample trilinear and anisotropic: give them mips (`core::MipLevelsFull` with `core::ImageHints::GenerateMipmaps`, which images decoded from files get by themselves).
- Local and Vulkan only, for now.

Why it is built this way - the queue, the records, the pass, the packs, the alternatives not taken and the open questions - is in [design/basic3d.adoc](../../design/basic3d.adoc).

## Making a scene

```cpp
class MyScene : public basic3d::Scene3d {
public:
	virtual bool init(NotNull<AppThread> app, NotNull<core::RenderServerChannel> window,
			const core::FrameConstraints &constraints) override {
		if (!Scene3d::init(app, window, constraints)) {
			return false;
		}
		auto content = Rc<basic3d::SceneContent3d>::create();
		auto view = content->addChild(Rc<basic3d::View3d>::create());
		view->setContentSize(Size2(800.0f, 600.0f));
		view->addDraw(basic3d::Draw3d{basic3d::Mesh3d::makeTorus(2.0f, 0.7f, 64, 24),
			Mat4::IDENTITY, basic3d::Material3d{Color4F::WHITE, texture, 1.0f}});
		setContent(content);
		return true;
	}
};
```

[`examples/window/basic3d`](../../../examples/window/basic3d/main.cpp) is a complete one: a spinning textured torus over a ground plane, two boxes drawn by a pipeline of the example's own, a translucent panel over all of them, mouse pan and wheel zoom on the view.

## Pipelines of the app

The stock mesh pipeline draws a material as its colour times its texture, lit by Lambert. A material can name another pipeline instead: one the app gives `Scene3d::init`.

```cpp
// shaders/my_terrain.vert and .frag, built by LOCAL_SHADERS_DIR in the app's Makefile; their
// preambles, glsl/my_terrain.*.glsl, from the app's schema on LOCAL_SHADERS_INCLUDE
#include "my_terrain.vert.h"
#include "my_terrain.frag.h"

Scene3d::init(app, window, constraints, Vector<basic3d::Pipeline3dInfo>{{"MyTerrain",
	SpanView<uint32_t>((const uint32_t *)my_terrain_vert, sizeof(my_terrain_vert) / 4),
	SpanView<uint32_t>((const uint32_t *)my_terrain_frag, sizeof(my_terrain_frag) / 4)}});

// the first of the app's pipelines is 1: 0 is the mesh pipeline
view->addDraw(Draw3d{mesh, Mat4::IDENTITY, Material3d{.texture = ground, .pipeline = 1,
	.flags = GridBit, .params = {Vec4(width, height, 0, 0), Vec4::ZERO}}});
```

- The shaders read what the mesh shaders read, from [`XL3dGlslData.h`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/glsl/include/XL3dGlslData.h): the push constants `Constants3d`, the vertexes, draws, views and lights they point to, the texture array at set 0, binding 0, and the layered one at binding 1. A draw's `flags`, `params`, `textureIdx2` and `textureIdx3` are the material's; a view's `screen` holds its size in pixels and the density, to turn widths in points into pixels, and `firstLight`/`lightCount` its lights in the frame's array.
- What a shader declares to read them comes from a schema of the app that imports basic3d's and names its shaders and their varyings ([xlkdl-gpu.adoc](../tools/xlkdl-gpu.adoc)): `xlkdl gpu` writes a preamble for each, and the shader includes it and writes `main()` - `vertices(i)`, `draws(constants.drawIndex)`, `view()`, `lights(i)`, the varyings as `vary.<name>`, a fragment shader's `outColor`. The preambles are committed; the app puts their directory on `LOCAL_SHADERS_INCLUDE`, not in `LOCAL_SHADERS_DIR`. The example's [basic3d-example.kdl](../../../examples/window/basic3d/glsl/basic3d-example.kdl) is one, and the mesh shaders, [`xl_3d_mesh.vert`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/glsl/shaders/xl_3d_mesh.vert) and `.frag`, are a starting point.
- A pipeline can read a material of its own instead of `flags` and `params`: a `material` of the app's schema, with fields of its own and textures of a known kind. The schema's header has a builder for it; a draw carries what it makes, and the pipeline names its schema:

  ```cpp
  #include "MyFieldData.h" // the app's schema's header

  auto water = Pipeline3dInfo{"MyWater", vert, frag, true, &WaterMaterial::Schema};
  view->addDraw(Draw3d{mesh, Mat4::IDENTITY, Material3d{.pipeline = 1,
  	.data = WaterMaterial{.ripples = normals, .time = t}.make()}});
  ```

  The shader reads it by `material()` (its schema's `shader … material=Water`). The frame writes a material once, however many draws share it - make it once and keep it, not once a draw - and patches the slots of its textures; a texture of the other kind than its field (a layered one in a `texture2d`) is none, with a warning. A draw of the pipeline without a material of that schema draws with the mesh pipeline, with one warning. `getLastPassStats()` counts the materials a frame wrote and their bytes. The example's glass is one ([basic3d-example.kdl](../../../examples/window/basic3d/glsl/basic3d-example.kdl)).
- Every pipeline tests depth Less and culls back faces; an opaque one writes depth.
- A pipeline with `translucent = true` draws after every opaque draw, in a second subpass of the pass: it blends over them by its alpha, does not write depth, and reads theirs as an input attachment at set 0, binding 2 - `sceneDepth` in the preamble of a fragment shader the schema marks `translucent=#true` - for how far the view goes through it (water's thickness). Its draws go in the order the view gives them: far to near, if they overlap on screen. The example's glass (`BASIC3D_GLASS=1`) is one.
- The SPIR-V is referenced, not copied: it has to outlive the scene, as embedded shader headers do.
- A material that picks a pipeline the scene does not have draws with the mesh pipeline, and the pass warns once. A pipeline with no name, a taken one or no shaders is refused at `init` with an error, and keeps its index.

The example's boxes are drawn by a pipeline of its own: stripes in the two colours of their params.

A view's camera, draws and lights change through its setters, which ask the window for a frame. A subclass with draws of its own (a terrain made of chunks) overrides `View3d::collectDraws`.

## How a frame gets to the GPU

1. `SceneContent3d` swaps basic2d's frame context for a [`FrameContext3d`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/XL3dFrameContext.h). Its handle is a `FrameContextHandle2d` with a list of views added, so every 2d node fills it as before.
2. `View3d::draw` sets its camera's viewport from its world rect and appends a `ViewInput3d`: the viewport, the camera's matrix and the lights, and the draws.
3. The context feeds the `Scene3dInput` attachment every frame, with no views when nothing was drawn: an input left unfed would stall the frame.
4. The [pass](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/backend/vk/XL3dVkPass.h) packs every view's meshes that are not in a loaded `MeshPack3d` into one vertex and one index buffer (a draw from a pack binds the pack's buffers instead; the view takes where they are on the app thread, `Draw3d::gpu`), with a buffer of draws and one of views, on a worker; the textures the draws use become the slots of its descriptor array. It draws before basic2d's pass, into the same `Output`, with a depth attachment of its own; basic2d loads what it drew.

Vertexes are read by `buffer_reference` through `gl_VertexIndex`; the layouts shared by C++ and GLSL are in [`XL3dGlslData.h`](../../../xenolith/renderer/basic3d/glsl/include/XL3dGlslData.h). Faces wind counter-clockwise seen from outside, and back faces are culled.

## Limits

- **No 2d children under a View3d.** They would be drawn as 2d content of the same frame, over everything.
- **64 textures a frame** (`basic3d::MaxTextures`) and **16 layered ones** (`MaxTextureArrays`). Draws past that show their colour alone, with a warning.
- **Layered textures are for app pipelines.** A texture with array layers takes a slot of the `sampler2DArray` array at binding 1, and the draw's index points there; the mesh pipeline samples none and draws such a material as its colour, with a warning.
- **Views share one depth buffer**: two views that overlap test against each other.
- **Local only.** A remote client's queue is the server's, and basic3d does not add its pass there.
- **Vulkan only.** There is no WebGPU, Metal, GLES or software pass yet.
- **The 2d damage is off.** `Scene3d` uses basic2d's Default queue with damage tracking off, because the 3d pass repaints the whole frame.

## Tests

- `tests/basic3d` (`basic3dtest`, no device): the camera's math, the mesh builders, the layout of the records the shaders read, a pack's layout.
- `tests/window/basic3d-check.py` drives the example headless: the torus drawn, textured and lit; the boxes of the app pipeline in the colours of their params and flags, one with a second and a third texture, one with a layer of a layered texture; a translucent glass blending over them, more where the depth behind it is further; the pool of a point light, gone without it; every draw from the mesh pack with no vertex copied into the frame, the same picture with `BASIC3D_NOPACK=1`, the pack leaving the cache once its draws are removed; a material with an unknown pipeline drawn as a mesh, with one warning; the panel over it; the ground's fine checker even through the mips and aliased with `BASIC3D_NOMIPS=1`; no validation error.
- `tests/window/pass3d-check.py` checks the engine features under the module - a pass under basic2d, culling, depth bias and clamp, two subpasses - with a hand-made pass.
